COMPLETED NUMERICAL PROGRAM

Four experiments with distinct mathematical responsibilities

The numerical program establishes a finite-speed storage advantage, resolves the associated counting fluctuations, and supplies independent exact and interval checks. Its strongest performance statement is a comparison between a continuously feasible assisted protocol and an upper bound covering every admissible frozen-storage protocol under the same gate schedule and resource specification. Its strongest protection statement is pathwise: signed auxiliary counts in a pendant tree are endpoint occupation differences and cannot accumulate winding. These statements are supported by different parts of the program, each designed around the mathematical quantity it evaluates.

Four protocol families appear in the evidence. Their separation is essential to interpreting the results. The exact local examples calculate curvature and friction at a uniform equilibrium. The harmonic examples follow those local designs into finite-speed operation. The resource-constrained examples solve a broader finite-speed control problem and then repair the proposed solution into a rational continuous protocol. The held-stage examples use exact rational rates and rigorous matrix exponentials to enclose finite-horizon probabilities. All four families use the same column-generator convention and graph orientation, but their periods, gate schedules, actuator spaces and kinetic budgets are explicitly different.

A finite-speed advantage beyond the strongest frozen-storage comparator

The continuous synthesis problem fixes the gate waveform and optimizes occupation and potential variables. The productive gate is a piecewise affine approximation to \(q_{01}=1+(5/8)\sin(2\pi t/T)\), with its knot values quantized to multiples of \(1/1024\). The remaining core conductances are one. A star or chain has unit conductance on each auxiliary edge. The auxiliary triangle uses the exact conductances \(683/1024,683/1024,682/1024\), whose sum is also two. Thus the installation budget is matched without introducing a rounded approximation to the repeating decimal \(2/3\).

\[ \begin{aligned} T&=8,& \Sigma&\le0.15,& \mathcal A&\le16,\\ |E_i|&\le1.2,& r_i^{\rm exit}&\le6,& |\dot E_i|&\le20,\\ p_i&\ge0.015,& \frac1T\int_0^T p_i(t)\,dt&=\frac15. \end{aligned} \]

The inventory condition fixes every state's mean occupation. The assistance cannot obtain its advantage by permanently moving a larger fraction of the population into a more favorable part of the graph. The activity budget counts all transitions in both directions, including auxiliary excursions. The escape ceiling prevents concentrating those transitions into arbitrarily fast bursts. The energy and slew limits bound the available well excursion and its speed. The baseline upper bound is valid even after relaxing its well-slew restriction, which enlarges the comparator class and strengthens the performance separation.

The optimization produces a proposal on 64 control intervals. Each proposed potential knot is converted into a rational number, contracted toward the positive constant \(3/16\) by the exact factor \(63/64\), and corrected by a constant vector. The correction makes the integrated probability increment exactly zero. A rational initial occupation then enforces exactly uniform mean inventory. On each interval the conductance and potential are affine, the current is quadratic, and the occupation is cubic. This permits exact polynomial identities for periodicity, normalization, mean inventory, current and activity. Bernstein bounds verify the pointwise polynomial inequalities. Outward-rounded Arb arithmetic encloses the logarithmic entropy integral and the reconstructed well energies and slew.

Local geometry remains predictive at finite driving speed

The harmonic experiment tests how much of the exact small-loop advantage survives under the original geometric design rule. Its control map is \(E=(0,\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)\), with the productive barrier \(B_{01}=\lambda_1\). A baseline plane uses \(\lambda_0,\lambda_1\); assistance uses all four coordinates. Each plane is optimal for the target curvature in the baseline friction metric. The axes are normalized by \(u^TGu=v^TGv=1\) and \(u^TGv=0\). A harmonic stroke has \(\lambda=a[u\cos(2\pi t/T)+v\sin(2\pi t/T)]\).

The baseline amplitude is 0.12. For the matched comparison, the assisted amplitude is obtained by a scalar root solve so that its full finite-speed entropy equals the baseline entropy. This matches the modeled dissipation along the actual periodic state, rather than matching only the leading quadratic action. It is a useful nonlinear benchmark, while the constrained compiler supplies the separate comparison that also controls total activity, rate and actuator limits.

The complete transport specification includes variance

For every retained finite-speed construction, the program computes both monitored means and their joint covariance. The tilted generator multiplies the forward and reverse rates of a counted edge by \(e^{\chi}\) and \(e^{-\chi}\), keeping its original diagonal escape rates. Derivative propagators evaluate the first two counting derivatives analytically:

\[ \begin{aligned} \dot M_0&=KM_0,\\ \dot M_a&=KM_a+K_aM_0,\\ \dot M_{ab}&=KM_{ab}+K_aM_b+K_bM_a+K_{ab}M_0. \end{aligned} \]

No subtraction of nearby tilted eigenvalues is required. Perron-eigenvector sensitivity supplies the long-time covariance per cycle, including correlations across periods. A separate backward periodic Poisson equation gives a martingale-corrector expression for the same covariance. The two calculations agree to better than \(3.5\times10^{-13}\) for the certified continuous protocols. The Floquet method follows the established periodic full-counting-statistics construction [Barato–Chetrite]; its independent Poisson check also tests the signs, count orientations and period boundary condition.

A finite acquisition can be specified directly. If \(M\) independent copies are observed for \(n\) periods, then the total mean is \(Mn\mu_L\) and its variance is \(M V_L(n)\), where \(V_L(n)\) is the retained finite-horizon variance. An SNR requirement \(z\) therefore needs

\[ M\ge\frac{z^2V_L(n)}{n^2\mu_L^2}. \]

At ten periods and SNR five, the three certified continuous protocols require 466 star copies, 430 chain copies or 440 triangle copies after rounding upward. For 1,000 chain copies over ten periods, the expected productive count is approximately 765.916, the count variance is approximately 10,085.056, and the expected microscopic activity is approximately 159,843.750 transitions. This gives a concrete workload: the useful signed count is extracted from a much larger two-way transition traffic. Independent copies improve statistical resolution while preserving that traffic accounting. A common externally prescribed waveform does not by itself correlate their thermal jump noise; interactions between copies would require an enlarged model.

The absorbing-count calculation answers a different question: has the auxiliary signed count ever left \([-1,1]\) during the observation? For the resource-constrained star and chain, survival is exactly one by topology. For the constructed auxiliary triangle, its computed survival after ten periods is 0.0586304718644; after one hundred periods it is approximately \(8.91\times10^{-14}\). The triangle's zero mean therefore coexists with substantial excursions. The tree architecture changes the support of the signed auxiliary count itself. It does not prevent occupation from entering the storage chamber.

The numerical Chernoff calculation also evaluates the finite-horizon target event \(N_L(10T)\le0\). For 1,000 independent copies of the same certified continuous protocol, the resulting upper estimates are \(2.25\times10^{-12}\) for the star, \(2.32\times10^{-13}\) for the chain and \(4.46\times10^{-13}\) for the auxiliary triangle. Bridge protection is also robust to uncertainty in the rates on the declared graph. The endpoint identity depends on which states an edge separates; it does not require accurately matched transition prefactors or a specially tuned relative barrier waveform. An additional unmodeled transition that closes a storage cycle changes that topology. The rare-shortcut calculation therefore treats extra transitions as an explicit hazard, with an observation-duration-dependent bound on the probability of any shortcut event. This distinguishes ordinary calibration error on an existing tree from a physical connection that creates a new winding route. The program's protection specification includes both the declared topology and the exposure horizon.

Verification, full precision and reproducible execution

The verification routes test independent mathematical structures. The tree audit retains 347 exact predicates covering rational local geometry, aggregate routing, resistance splitting and bridge observables. The frozen-noise audit checks fifteen exact models with 210 rational identities, including equality of the frozen covariance, Poisson expression and cycle floor. The smooth statistics program passes 81 checks. Independent review reconstructs the graph floor directly from cycle resistances for all nine harmonic cases. The final compiled-protocol statistics add 23 passing checks, including exact-source mean and activity replay, periodic occupation, work–entropy agreement, Poisson–Floquet agreement and finite-horizon bridge variance.

The feasibility certificate and statistics certificate have different roles. The former proves the rational source current and validates continuous resource limits. The latter independently integrates the reconstructed generator and computes its counting distribution. The held-stage probability study adds 34 passing interval and reference checks. The numerical source is organized by responsibility. The exact tree and noise modules reproduce symbolic statements. The convex compiler proposes protocols and the dual module bounds the continuous comparator. The primal certificate converts the proposal into a continuously feasible rational construction. The precision module replays the exact rational held stages and probability enclosures. Each module writes structured evidence that can be inspected without rerunning the visual presentation.

The result is an operationally stronger form of Cyclecraft. A storage chamber can now be selected for a certified finite-speed improvement, its allowed excursions and microscopic traffic can be budgeted, and its accumulated signed counting behavior can be specified. The constructive examples establish that acyclic storage can preserve a substantial assistance benefit while giving exact support control of auxiliary signed counts. The remaining productive cycle carries the useful transport and its measurable noise; the auxiliary states supply controlled memory without adding another winding coordinate.
