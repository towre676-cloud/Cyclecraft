A circulation core with controlled storage

The engineering object is a small thermal routing machine with a productive cycle and an attached store of occupation. Its output is the expected signed number of completed transitions through a designated core edge during a prescribed period. The storage states exchange probability with the junction, changing when material is available to the active cycle. A branch provides this freedom without introducing a second winding coordinate. The architecture therefore assigns different physical jobs to different graph structures: the core supports circulation, while the auxiliary tree supports controlled storage and release.

The completed five-state comparison makes this separation explicit. States 0, 1 and 2 form the active triangle. States 3 and 4 form either two leaves attached to state 0, a chain attached through state 0, or the original auxiliary triangle. The productive core and the number of states are common. Total installed auxiliary conductance is also common: the two tree edges have unit conductance, while the three cyclic auxiliary conductances sum exactly to two in the declared rational construction. Thus a comparison can distinguish the effects of storage connectivity from the simple addition of transition capacity.

The branch theorem is particularly valuable because its protection is structural. For a bridge edge, the signed count is the change in the indicator of the component beyond that edge. A particle may visit the store repeatedly; those forward and reverse crossings cancel except for its final storage location. Every auxiliary edge of the two tree architectures has this property. It holds under changes of well energies, gate values and driving speed, as long as the graph retains its tree connection. The auxiliary triangle instead has a genuine cycle coordinate. Its fixed-barrier fence removes mean drift in that coordinate, while the full counting calculation retains its fluctuations.

The exact aggregate-routing identity explains why eliminating auxiliary winding need not eliminate assistance. Let \(P_0=p_0+p_3+p_4\), with \(P_1=p_1\) and \(P_2=p_2\). For any auxiliary network attached solely through state 0,

\[ j_{\mathrm{core}}=H_{\mathrm{core}}(q_{\mathrm{core}})\dot P, \qquad \mathcal Q_{\mathrm{core}}=\oint H_{\mathrm{core}}\,dP. \]

Storage affects the path \(P(t)\) and the resources needed to realize it. The core routing map itself is unchanged at a fixed aggregate path. This is an exact statement about mean currents, rather than an assumption that the aggregated process is Markovian. Relaxation and fluctuations still depend on the full five-state dynamics. Keeping that distinction preserves the useful reduction without discarding the memory created by internal storage states.

The operating envelope is part of the design

The constrained synthesis uses period 8, entropy ceiling 0.15, total expected transition count at most 16, absolute well-energy limit 1.2, escape-rate ceiling 6 and well-energy slew ceiling 20. Energies are in bath thermal units and time is in the declared kinetic unit. The occupation floor is 0.015. Each state has mean occupation 0.2 over the period. These inventory constraints prevent a design from obtaining its comparison simply by permanently concentrating most probability in a convenient subset of states. They also make the five-state stores comparable as controlled distributions of the same total material.

\[ \frac1T\int_0^T p_i(t)\,dt=\frac15, \qquad \mathcal A=\int_0^T\sum_e \left[k_{j\leftarrow i}p_i+k_{i\leftarrow j}p_j\right]dt\le16. \]

Total activity counts all forward and backward transitions, including every visit to storage and every recrossing that contributes zero net output. It is consequently a useful kinetic budget alongside entropy. A favorable circulation-to-entropy ratio alone can conceal a large number of microscopic transitions. Activity-constrained Markov transport already establishes the need to specify this resource when minimizing dissipation [Dechant]. The present compiler retains the commanded circulation and fixed gate schedule while enforcing that budget on the complete network.

The escape ceiling controls how the transition budget is spent. Without it, a protocol might place a large fraction of its activity in a brief, very fast episode. The energy box limits the available equilibrium preferences. The slew ceiling limits how rapidly those preferences can move. These controls address different physical restrictions and are retained separately in the record. Their combination describes a family of admissible machines, rather than a single normalization of an otherwise unrestricted stroke.

The average-inventory constraint has a practical interpretation when several identical carriers or repeated preparations are used. Each labeled state must receive its declared share of residence over the complete command, so storage assistance is evaluated through the timing of occupation rather than a permanent change of stock. This is useful for a cyclic processing step whose preparation and recovery conditions must repeat. A different application can prescribe a nonuniform inventory vector instead, but that vector must be common to the compared designs. Its selection is part of the device specification, alongside the counted route and operating period.

The frozen-storage comparator retains the same auxiliary states, auxiliary edges, gate schedule and resource envelope. Its auxiliary wells are fixed, while its active wells may be controlled throughout the period. The assisted machine releases the auxiliary well controls into the same finite energy range. The continuous upper certificate bounds the entire declared frozen-storage class. In particular, allowing a relaxation of its slew restriction can only enlarge that class. An assisted waveform that satisfies the actual slew ceiling and exceeds this enlarged comparator bound establishes a strict finite-speed advantage without requiring a global solution of the full assisted optimization problem.

This is the proper interpretation of the certified gap. It applies to the prescribed conductance schedule and the stated inventory and resource constraints. The gate waveform is selected before the comparison and is shared by the machines. Simultaneously optimizing every conductance, topology and waveform is a larger problem. The current result supplies a strong, reproducible design statement inside a transparent operating envelope, with a feasible continuous construction on one side and a validated continuum upper bound on the other.

From a calibrated landscape to an executable command

A prospective physical implementation can use a controllable multiwell landscape: each well defines a mesoscopic state, and thermally activated transitions define the graph edges. Optical, electrical, mechanical or chemical control would set the well depths and selected transition barriers. The finite-state model is then a calibrated reduction of the underlying dynamics. Metastable transition theory relates escape times to barrier structure and local geometry; its prefactors and validity conditions matter when translating a conductance into an actual rate [Berglund–Gentz]. The completed results here concern the finite-state model and its numerical realization.

The calibration task is concrete. Identify states with repeatable residence behavior, estimate forward and reverse transition rates at frozen settings, determine which settings change each gate, and test whether the frozen rate ratios are consistent with a common set of state energies. Reversibility requires zero affinity around every frozen graph cycle:

\[ \sum_{e=(i,j)\in\Gamma} \log\frac{k_{j\leftarrow i}}{k_{i\leftarrow j}}=0. \]

For the Arrhenius form \(k_{j\leftarrow i}=q_e e^{E_i}\), this identity follows by cancellation of energy differences. It is also an experimentally meaningful check on state reduction. A persistent cycle affinity indicates an additional drive, a hidden state or a calibration discrepancy. Such a system requires an extended current description containing a stationary cycle contribution. The exact occupation–barrier current used in this paper applies to the frozen-reversible model.

The control compiler returns a probability path and positive variables \(x_i\), from which the well command follows directly:

\[ E_i(t)=\log\frac{x_i(t)}{p_i(t)},\qquad \dot p=-L(t)x, \qquad k_{j\leftarrow i}(t)=q_e(t)\frac{x_i(t)}{p_i(t)}. \]

The validated occupation floor keeps this reconstruction finite. The polynomial and interval checks establish the bounds between control knots, including positivity, energy range, escape rate and slew. Reintegrating the original master equation independently checks that the reconstructed command produces the designed path. The implemented object is therefore a continuous waveform with an explicit mathematical representation, rather than a collection of unconstrained numerical samples.

The fixed gate schedule is particularly useful for a first implementation because it separates routing calibration from occupation control. A device can repeat the same gate command while different well commands explore the feasible performance frontier. The baseline and assisted experiments then share the routing hardware and its timing. Static conductance calibration also records manufacturing variation instead of assuming identical channels. The mathematical certificate is evaluated for those specified conductances; subsequent experiments can sweep bounded calibration uncertainty around them.

The modeled entropy accounts for well-modulation work over a periodic state. A physical controller may expend additional energy in operating gates, electronics, optics or chemical delivery. Those expenditures belong in a device-specific budget. Using the same gate schedule in the comparison makes the present result especially suitable for such an extension: the additional driver model can be attached to a fixed routing command while the well-control contribution remains explicit. Energy range and slew provide useful implementation constraints, but do not by themselves replace a measured actuator-power model.

Occupation measurements can recover mean circulation

Once the relative conductances are calibrated, occupation measurements provide a second interface to the machine. The exact identity \(j=H(q)\dot p\) reconstructs the mean edge current from the occupation history. The common multiplicative kinetic scale cancels from \(H\). When individual transitions cannot be resolved but an ensemble occupation can be imaged or counted, the integrated form avoids direct numerical differentiation:

\[ \widehat{\mathcal Q} =\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}H(q(t_k)) \left[\widehat p(t_{k+1})-\widehat p(t_k)\right]. \]

Summation by parts expresses occupation-noise sensitivity through the size and variation of the routing map. Smooth gate commands therefore support both stable actuation and stable inference. The measurement is model-assisted: continuity by itself does not determine cycle current. The calibrated reversible routing law supplies the missing relation. A complete acquisition record should retain state definitions, the relative conductance schedule, sampling times and endpoint occupations alongside the inferred current.

Specify the useful output and its observation horizon

A pump specification needs a horizon and a counted event. The mean productive count per period describes direction and throughput, while its covariance describes how much averaging is required to resolve that mean. The finite-horizon quantities used here are obtained from the actual periodic propagator and its counting derivatives. They include interperiod correlations. Floquet counting statistics gives the corresponding long-time cumulants [Barato–Chetrite]; the finite-period propagation is retained when a specified observation lasts only one, ten or one hundred periods.

For \(n\) periods and \(M\) independent copies of a device, let \(m_n\) and \(v_n\) be the mean and variance of the productive count for one copy. Independence gives

\[ \mathbb E N_{\mathrm{total}}=M m_n, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(N_{\mathrm{total}})=M v_n, \qquad \mathrm{SNR}=\sqrt M\,\frac{|m_n|}{\sqrt{v_n}}. \]

This converts a model-level precision improvement into fewer parallel copies or a shorter acquisition. The independence assumption is explicit: interacting carriers, a shared fluctuating control field or a common measurement disturbance can introduce cross-copy covariance. The single-particle program does not silently suppress those effects. Instead, it supplies the elementary device law to which an interaction or common-noise model can be added.

For a probability requirement, the generating function is more informative than an SNR alone. With the tilted one-period propagator \(M_\theta\),

\[ G_n(\theta)=\mathbf1^T M_\theta^n p_0, \qquad \Pr[N_{\mathrm{total}}\le a] \le e^{s a}\,G_n(-s)^M, \qquad s>0. \]

A chosen tilt produces a finite-time upper bound without assuming a Gaussian count distribution. The rigorous held-protocol example evaluates such quantities with ball matrix exponentials, exact rational source rates and an enclosed periodic initialization. It provides a distinct reliability calculation alongside the smooth resource-constrained synthesis. The distinction is operational: the held example demonstrates a certified probability calculation for an explicit command, while the smooth example establishes the bounded-resource mean advantage.

Auxiliary count requirements should distinguish terminal counts from an event that occurs at any time. Opposite windings can cancel by the end of an experiment. To compute the probability of ever exceeding a tolerance, the state is lifted to the pair \((i,k)\), where \(i\) is the physical state and \(k\) the accumulated signed count. Transitions that leave the allowed count interval are absorbed. The surviving probability is the sum of the substochastic propagator applied to the initial law. This construction directly answers a first-exceedance specification.

For an ideal storage tree and a bridge tolerance of one, the pathwise theorem gives an exact structural zero for that exceedance event. It does not prohibit visits to the storage chamber. A visit changes both the occupation indicator and the signed bridge count; a return reverses that change. The same statement also does not make gross traffic free: every visit and return enters the activity budget and the entropy accounting. The useful protection is a bound on the accumulated signed observable for every trajectory.

Quantify accidental closing transitions

A manufactured or coarse-grained tree can contain a weak additional transition that closes a loop. The reliability question then becomes quantitative. Suppose the total hazard of using any such closing transition is bounded by \(r_{\mathrm{close}}(t)\) throughout the state space. Every newly possible auxiliary winding requires at least one closing-edge jump, giving

\[ \Pr[\text{new auxiliary winding by }\tau] \le\Pr[\text{a closing jump by }\tau] \le1-\exp\!\left[-\int_0^\tau r_{\mathrm{close}}(t)\,dt\right]. \]

The bound turns an unresolved shortcut rate into a finite operating-horizon requirement. A calibrated upper rate and a chosen horizon suffice to give a conservative acceptance threshold. The explicit rare-edge calculations also evaluate the actual shortcut-jump probability for the declared model, revealing how conservative the uniform hazard bound is. This supplies an engineering reason to search for weak cross-connections during calibration, rather than treating nominal tree topology as an untested manufacturing assumption.

The next mathematical questions have precise interfaces

The completed compiler fixes the gate waveform and optimizes occupation control. Releasing the gates creates the next major optimization problem because products \(q_e x_i\) couple two design variables. The present convex structure then becomes a certified inner problem and a source of outer bounds, rather than a global solution of arbitrary gate design. A useful extension would combine a small, physically specified family of smooth gate waveforms with exact inner certificates, then establish a uniform gain over a region of resource budgets and calibration uncertainty.

Multiple attachment ports create a second structural frontier. A storage graph attached through one junction has the exact aggregate-routing reduction used here. Connecting it to two core locations introduces new routes and can create additional cycles. The design problem then concerns a boundary response operator with internal memory. Its useful outputs should include the accessible circulation space, the storage-induced relaxation modes and the count covariance on the enlarged cycle space. This is a natural place to combine graph elimination with the present occupation calculus, while keeping the full stochastic realization available for precision calculations.

A resisting load provides another clear device extension. Useful work becomes the load affinity times productive signed transport, and the periodic entropy balance subtracts that output from well-modulation input. The current generally acquires a frozen stationary component in addition to the occupation-induced contribution. An unbiased tree store still has bounded bridge counts by topology, but its assistance must be judged through stall load, output power, efficiency and fluctuations under the same resource limits. Those quantities provide a concrete experiment beyond unloaded circulation.

Finally, interacting particles require a configuration-state graph or a controlled approximation to it. Exclusion, finite storage capacity and common actuation can alter both assistance and noise. The one-particle construction supplies an exact reference against which those changes can be measured. Existing full-counting-statistics optimal control already demonstrates that means and noise can be shaped jointly [Impens et al.]. The distinctive continuation is to preserve Cyclecraft’s exact routing structure and physical budgets while adding those interactions. The current result establishes the elementary design principle on which that continuation can build: controlled storage can improve productive circulation, and an acyclic store can do so without adding an accumulating auxiliary winding count.
