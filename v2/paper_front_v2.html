Abstract

We develop a design calculus for thermal circulation with auxiliary storage, combining exact graph geometry, finite-speed convex synthesis, trajectory statistics and validated numerical bounds. The central architectural separation is between the graph cycles that carry productive transport and the branches that supply controllable occupation. For a storage network attached through one junction, the active current depends only on its aggregated occupation path. For a tree, each signed storage-edge count is exactly an endpoint indicator difference and lies in \(\{-1,0,1\}\) on every particle trajectory. The auxiliary chamber can therefore change the productive current without introducing an additional accumulating winding variable.

Exact rational examples establish strict local assistance. A triangle with one controlled storage leaf increases its optimal curvature–action coefficient by \(\sqrt{66}/5-1=62.480768\ldots\%\). Five-state leaf and chain examples give further exact benchmarks. We then formulate finite-speed synthesis in occupation variables \((p,x)\), where \(x_i=e^{E_i}p_i\): the dynamics and current are linear, entropy is convex, and energy, rate and total transition-activity limits become linear constraints. A continuous-time dual yields an upper bound for the entire frozen-storage comparator class. Exact rational repair and interval analysis yield a feasible assisted construction.

At period eight, with entropy at most 0.15, total activity at most sixteen, well energies within \(\pm1.2\), escape rates at most six, a positive probability floor and equal mean inventories, the certified two-leaf current is 0.07369813425630575 per cycle. The corresponding baseline upper bound is 0.06423038232315985. The certified advantage is at least 14.74%; chain and cyclic auxiliary constructions also exceed their respective baseline bounds. The assisted well slew is bounded, while the comparator upper bounds even admit unlimited well slew.

Counting statistics distinguish bounded storage counts from productive-current uncertainty. Periodic Poisson correctors give the exact asymptotic covariance and a graph-theoretic noise floor. Floquet derivative equations give finite-horizon moments. Separate explicit three-hold protocols have rigorous 320-bit ball-arithmetic enclosures and an independent 110-digit reference. Over ten periods, the cyclic auxiliary exceeds a signed-count tolerance of one with probability 0.74909010509236, while a storage bridge obeys the tolerance pathwise. Rare closing edges receive explicit hazard bounds. The complete numerical sources, exact certificates and autonomous figures accompany the manuscript.

The design proposition

A useful auxiliary chamber need not circulate. It can instead exchange occupation with a productive cycle in a controlled sequence. The distinction is physically consequential. Circulation is associated with graph homology: a particle can complete repeated windings, and their signed count can grow without bound. Storage on a branch is associated with an endpoint observable: a particle enters a component or leaves it, and the difference of those events is its current membership. These two graph roles can be designed separately.

The first Cyclecraft construction used a barrier fence to suppress the mean circulation of a second loop while allowing its well controls to improve the first loop. The present construction identifies the occupation mechanism directly. A single attachment permits the storage mass to be aggregated into the junction for the purpose of computing active mean current. The same reduction applies to a cyclic chamber, a chain and a collection of leaves. Their productive routing is identical for a fixed aggregated occupation curve. Their energetic cost, controllability and fluctuations differ. This converts storage topology into a meaningful design variable.

The resulting design question is exact: can an assisted protocol deliver more productive circulation than every frozen-storage protocol allowed the same gate schedule, resource budgets and mean inventory? The paper answers this question affirmatively for explicit five-state models. It supplies a feasible assisted waveform and a continuous-time comparator bound. The numerical optimizer proposes the waveform and dual variables; exact polynomial identities and outward-rounded inequalities decide the claim.

The comparison uses a fixed positive gate waveform. This is an experimentally meaningful class: a fabrication or external gate signal may be predetermined, while the well energies remain available for control. The baseline has all active well controls, with the two auxiliary wells frozen. Assistance releases the auxiliary well controls under the same amplitude, rate, entropy and activity limits. The mean occupation of every state is fixed at one fifth, so the advantage concerns cyclic use of storage rather than permanent removal of the auxiliary inventory. The baseline upper bound allows an even larger class by omitting its well-slew restriction. The constructed assisted waveform satisfies the finite slew bound.

What is certified

For the declared gate schedule and common resource class, an assisted feasible current lies strictly above an upper bound for all continuous-time frozen-storage controls. This is a conditional global comparison within the stated model. It is stronger than comparing two selected driving loops. It also leaves the productive current’s fluctuations visible: a bounded auxiliary count does not make the useful output deterministic.

Geometric pumping, microscopic cycle thermodynamics, Markov entropy geometry and full counting statistics provide the foundations. Their combination here serves a specific control architecture. Established optimal-control work already treats mean transport and noise jointly; activity-constrained transport already identifies the cost of arbitrarily fast rates. [Impens et al.][Dechant] The present result couples those concerns to a prescribed reversible routing law, cyclic occupation constraints, a storage topology with pathwise count bounds and continuous numerical certificates.

The relevant engineering output is consequently a specification rather than a single efficiency number: a productive current, an entropy ceiling, a count of all expected transitions, finite actuator limits, a storage-count guarantee and an observation-dependent precision. Every one of these quantities has an equation and a numerical object behind it. This is the common language used by the theory, source program and figures.

Ten equations, one compiler

The ten-equation selection is retained as a working architecture. Each equation supplies a different operation; the new results arise from composing those operations under one graph and sign convention. Energies are measured in bath thermal units. The master equation uses column probabilities. Each undirected edge is given one orientation, which fixes the sign of its current.
